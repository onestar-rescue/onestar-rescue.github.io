How to fix repeat bad Google reviews (not just reply to them)

Blog · November 5, 2026

If you run the counter yourself, the low reviews usually repeat: nobody called, the wait had no update, or the final price was not the quote. A public reply shows the next person that you read it. It does not stop the same visit from happening again.

Work in this order. It fits auto repair, restaurants, food trucks, cafes, HVAC, and plumbing. Read the lowest reviews and group them. Write one calm public reply. Send a private win-back that never asks the customer to change or remove the review. Put a counter script, a kitchen script, or a customer-update text where that complaint starts.

Replies in Google Business Profile are free, and you can send the private note from your own phone. OneStar Rescue is not affiliated with or endorsed by Google. Do not remove reviews, buy them, or solicit them.

Read the lowest reviews for the pattern

Open the listing, sort by lowest rating, and read the 1-star, 2-star, and 3-star reviews from the last 12 to 24 months. Write one line per review in the customer’s words, plus the date. “Called at 3 and still had no idea” is a missed update. “More than the estimate” is a price change. “Said 2 to 4 and showed up after 6” is a missed arrival window. If you only record the star, every reply sounds the same.

A notes app is enough. With only one or two low reviews, answer them and group later. Mark whether you already replied, and whether the name matches an order or a job. Only a matched name gets a private message. Do not invent an email from a first name.

Group the repeats by theme

Two or three piles is enough to act on. Auto repair usually sorts into no update while the car was in the bay, a price that changed after the estimate, or a car that missed the time you gave. Restaurants, food trucks, and cafes sort into a long wait with no word, a wrong order, or a call nobody returned. HVAC and plumbing sort into a missed arrival window, a charge the customer did not expect, or a finished job with no next step explained.

Start with the largest pile. A review can sit in two piles when someone hit two problems, and those need two sentences. Keep the quote and the date. “Four reviews since March say we never texted” is a task for the counter. An average star rating is not.

Write one calm public reply

Reply on the review in your Google Business Profile. Thank them, name the issue they wrote, leave a number that reaches you, and ask them to call so you can take it offline. Sign your name. Say what changed only if that change is already in use. Do not invent a fix for the reply.

Fill this in, then rewrite the middle line for the actual complaint:

Thanks for writing this. Waiting all afternoon with no update is not acceptable. Call me at [your number] and I will look into your visit. [Your name], owner

Name their complaint in that middle line: the wait, the price, the wrong order, the missed window. Do not paste one paragraph under every review, and do not argue about the star. Do not write that you already text updates, restate a price before extra work, or remake an order unless that is already happening at the counter. Those lines belong in the script below, and they belong in a public reply only after that script is in use. Answer unanswered reviews first. Google does not charge for the reply box.

Send a private win-back that never asks for an edit

The public reply is for the next customer. The private note is for this one. Send it from your own phone, in your name, only when the review matches a visit you can find.

Thank them and name the issue. Offer to talk, or to redo the work, only if you mean it. Add what you changed only when that change is already in use. Otherwise leave that line out. Do not ask them to change the review or remove it. Do not tie a discount, a free meal, a refund, or a redo to an edit, including a soft “an updated review would mean a lot.”

We do not write, buy, or solicit reviews, and a win-back is never conditional on changing or removing one. That follows Google’s review policy and the FTC’s rule on consumer reviews. If the name does not match a visit, do not go looking for a number. The public reply is the contact.

Put a script or an update text where the complaint starts

The Google reply reaches people who already had a bad visit. A script reaches the person at the counter, the window, or the door tomorrow. Write it for the largest pile.

  • Auto repair, “nobody called.” Text at drop-off with the time you will report back. Text the finding and the price, and wait for a yes before extra work. Text when the car is ready and repeat the approved price.
  • Restaurant, food truck, or cafe. Say the wait before they have to ask. Keep a kitchen or window line for a wrong order: what was wrong, that you are remaking it, and that there is no second charge.
  • HVAC or plumbing. Text when you leave the previous job so the arrival window is a real time. Say the price before the work starts.

Leave those lines where the Saturday closer can read them. If the wording stays in your head, the rush will invent a different one. Do not tell a reviewer you already send these texts until the closer is actually sending them. A month later, sort new low reviews into the same piles. If the biggest pile did not shrink, the script is not in use or it is aimed at the wrong complaint.

What to leave alone

Do not try to get reviews removed, and do not pay anyone who offers to. Do not buy reviews. Do not ask for one in exchange for a coupon, a sooner slot, or a free item. Do not hand the review link only to people who just said they were pleased.

Do not promise a star rating, a place in search, or that any review will come down. You control the update text, the price you say before the work, and whether you answer the phone.
